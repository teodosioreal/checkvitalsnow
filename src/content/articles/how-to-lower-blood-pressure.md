Can you really change your blood pressure numbers with everyday habits, or is a prescription the only answer?

For many people, habits can make a real difference. **To lower blood pressure, health agencies recommend a heart-healthy eating plan such as DASH, less sodium, regular physical activity, limiting alcohol, reaching a healthy weight, getting enough sleep, and not smoking.** These changes may help bring numbers down on their own for some people, and they can help medicine do its job for others. Whether you need medicine is a decision to make with your doctor.

If you just got a high reading, you're in a very common spot, and you likely feel fine, which is typical (our guide to [high blood pressure symptoms](/heart-health/high-blood-pressure-symptoms/) explains why). Below, you'll find what each habit involves, where people tend to get stuck, and a four-week plan that adds one change at a time.

## How to lower blood pressure: seven habits health agencies agree on

The NHLBI and MedlinePlus list a similar set of heart-healthy lifestyle changes for managing blood pressure. Here they are in plain terms:

| Habit | What it looks like in daily life |
| --- | --- |
| **Follow a heart-healthy eating plan** | More vegetables, fruits, whole grains, beans, and low-fat dairy. The DASH plan is built for this |
| **Eat less sodium** | Fewer packaged, restaurant, and fast foods, where most sodium hides |
| **Move more** | Brisk walking, cycling, swimming, or anything that gets your heart going, most days |
| **Limit alcohol** | Fewer drinks, fewer days. If you don't drink, there's no reason to start |
| **Aim for a healthy weight** | Even a modest loss may help if you carry extra weight |
| **Sleep enough** | Regular, good-quality sleep. Snoring with pauses in breathing is worth mentioning to a doctor |
| **Don't smoke** | Quitting helps your heart and blood vessels in many ways |

Stress management also appears on many agency lists. Ongoing stress can lead to habits that raise blood pressure, like poor sleep, drinking, and skipping exercise, so finding a calm routine can support everything else.

## The DASH eating plan for lower blood pressure

DASH stands for Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension. It comes from NHLBI-funded research and is one of the eating plans most often recommended for blood pressure.

DASH isn't a strict diet with forbidden foods. It's a pattern. The NHLBI describes it as:

- **Plenty of:** vegetables, fruits, and whole grains
- **Including:** fat-free or low-fat dairy, fish, poultry, beans, nuts, seeds, and vegetable oils
- **Limiting:** foods high in saturated fat (such as fatty meats, full-fat dairy, and tropical oils like coconut and palm oil), sugar-sweetened drinks, and sweets
- **Keeping sodium in check**

### Easy DASH-style swaps

| Instead of... | Try... |
| --- | --- |
| Chips with lunch | A piece of fruit or raw vegetables |
| White bread or white rice | Whole-grain bread, brown rice, or oats |
| Deli meats and hot dogs | Home-cooked chicken, fish, or beans |
| Soda or sweet tea | Water, sparkling water, or unsweetened tea |
| Salt at the table | Herbs, spices, lemon, garlic, or pepper |
| Canned soup with regular sodium | Lower-sodium versions, or homemade soup |

## Cutting sodium: where the salt really comes from

The AHA recommends **no more than 2,300 milligrams of sodium a day** for most adults, with an **ideal limit of 1,500 mg**, especially for people with high blood pressure. Most Americans eat well over that.

The saltshaker is rarely the main source. The AHA points out that most of the sodium people eat comes from packaged and restaurant foods. That means the biggest wins often come from:

- **Reading the Nutrition Facts label** and comparing brands. The "% Daily Value" for sodium is a quick guide: lower is better.
- **Cooking at home more often**, even a few extra meals a week.
- **Watching the usual suspects:** breads and rolls, pizza, sandwiches, cold cuts, soups, and savory snacks.
- **Asking for sauces and dressings on the side** when you eat out.

Your taste buds adjust over a few weeks. Foods that tasted bland at first often start to taste normal.

## Moving more, without a gym membership

The CDC recommends that adults get at least **150 minutes of moderate-intensity activity a week**, such as brisk walking, plus muscle-strengthening activity on two or more days. That can look like 30 minutes, five days a week, or shorter blocks spread through the day.

If you're starting from zero, start small. A 10-minute walk after dinner counts. If you have heart disease, chest pain with activity, or another health condition, check with your doctor before starting something more intense.

## Paths by profile: lowering blood pressure in your situation

### If you just learned your blood pressure is elevated or Stage 1

This is the best moment to build habits. Ask your doctor whether lifestyle changes alone are a reasonable first step for you, and when to recheck. Our [blood pressure chart](/heart-health/blood-pressure-readings-chart/) explains what each category means.

### If you already take blood pressure medicine

Keep taking it exactly as prescribed. Lifestyle changes go hand in hand with medicine, and they may help you need less of it over time, but only your doctor can make that call. **Never stop or lower a dose on your own**, even if your readings look great. Normal numbers often mean the medicine is doing its job.

### If you also have high cholesterol or diabetes

Many of the same habits help with all three. Ask your doctor about personal targets. Our guide to [high cholesterol numbers](/heart-health/high-cholesterol-numbers/) explains what your lab results mean.

### If you're thinking about supplements

The NHLBI's treatment advice centers on lifestyle and, when needed, prescription medicine. Some supplements and over-the-counter products, including certain decongestants and pain relievers, can raise blood pressure or interact with medicines. Ask your doctor or pharmacist before you take anything new.

## A 4-week blood pressure habit plan

Trying to change everything at once usually backfires. This plan adds one habit a week and keeps the earlier ones going. Track your home readings throughout, using the routine in our [blood pressure chart guide](/heart-health/blood-pressure-readings-chart/).

| Week | New habit | One concrete action | Check-in question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Know your sodium | Read the label on 5 foods you eat often. Swap one high-sodium food for a lower-sodium version | Which food surprised me most? |
| 2 | Add a walk | Walk briskly for 10 to 15 minutes on at least 5 days. After a meal works well | Did I hit 5 days? What got in the way? |
| 3 | Fill half the plate | Make half of lunch and dinner vegetables or fruit. Swap one refined grain for a whole grain | What vegetable is easiest for me? |
| 4 | Protect your evening | Set a regular bedtime. Cut back on alcohol, especially on weeknights | How did I sleep this week? |

After week four, look at your log. Lifestyle changes often take a few weeks or more to show in your numbers, so keep going even if they haven't moved yet. Share the log with your doctor at your next visit.

## Blood pressure warning signs while you make changes

Lifestyle changes are for steady, everyday blood pressure. Some situations need medical help instead:

- **Call 911** if your reading is higher than 180/120 on a second check and you have chest pain, shortness of breath, back pain, numbness or weakness, changes in vision, or difficulty speaking.
- **Call your doctor right away** if your reading stays above 180/120 without those symptoms.
- **Call your doctor** if you feel dizzy, lightheaded, or faint after starting or changing a blood pressure medicine, or if your home readings stay high despite treatment.
- **Get emergency help** for signs of a heart attack or stroke, whatever your reading. Our guide to [heart attack warning signs](/heart-health/heart-attack-warning-signs/) lists what to watch for.

## Your first blood pressure change, starting today

Pick the one food you eat most often that comes in a package, a bread, a soup, a snack, and check its sodium on the label today. If there's a lower-sodium version, buy that one next time. It's a small step, and it's the first box in your four-week plan.
