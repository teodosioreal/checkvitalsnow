Is it low testosterone, or is it just turning 50 with a desk job, two kids, and five hours of sleep a night? That's the question behind most searches for low T, and it's a fair one. The symptoms overlap with ordinary tiredness so much that guessing rarely works.

**Common low testosterone symptoms include a lower sex drive, erection problems, low energy, loss of muscle, more body fat, and changes in mood or focus. Because poor sleep, stress, weight gain, and other conditions cause the same signs, the only way to know is a blood test, usually done in the morning and repeated.**

Wondering about this is common and nothing to be embarrassed about. Asking a doctor about low testosterone is a routine conversation, and the answer is often more useful than you expect, whether or not your level turns out to be low.

## Low T symptoms versus ordinary aging

Testosterone peaks in early adulthood and then declines slowly. Mayo Clinic describes this gradual drop as a normal part of aging, and many men never notice it. Low testosterone that causes symptoms, called male hypogonadism, is a medical condition with a cause that's worth finding.

The table below lists signs that health organizations such as Cleveland Clinic and the Urology Care Foundation link to low testosterone, next to the everyday explanations that often cause the same thing.

| Symptom | Can point to low testosterone | Other common explanations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lower sex drive | Yes, one of the most typical signs | Stress, relationship strain, depression, some medicines, poor sleep |
| Erection problems | Can contribute | Blood vessel disease, diabetes, medicines, anxiety. See our guide to [erectile dysfunction causes](/mens-health/erectile-dysfunction-causes/) |
| Low energy, tiredness | Possible | Short sleep, sleep apnea, thyroid problems, anemia, depression |
| Less muscle, more body fat | Possible | Less activity, diet changes, aging itself |
| Low mood, irritability, poor focus | Possible | Depression, anxiety, burnout |
| Fewer morning erections | Can point to it | Poor sleep, alcohol |
| Breast tenderness or growth | Can happen | Some medicines, weight gain, other hormone problems |
| Loss of body hair, hot flashes, smaller testicles | More specific signs | Less common otherwise. Mention them to a doctor |

Notice how often the right column wins. That doesn't make your symptoms less real. It means a good doctor will look at the whole picture, not only one number.

## How a testosterone blood test works

MedlinePlus explains that testosterone is measured with a simple blood draw from a vein in your arm. A few details matter more than most men realize.

- **Timing.** Testosterone is usually highest in the morning, so doctors typically order the test early in the day.
- **Repeat testing.** Levels move around from day to day. A single low result is often rechecked on another morning before anyone calls it low testosterone.
- **More than one number.** Your doctor may also check other hormones, such as those made by the pituitary gland, to find out where the problem starts.
- **Your medicines matter.** Some medicines and supplements can affect results. Bring a full list, including anything you buy without a prescription.

Lab reference ranges vary, and a "normal" range on the report doesn't decide treatment on its own. Your doctor combines your symptoms with repeated test results.

## What can cause low testosterone

Finding the cause matters, because sometimes treating it is the whole answer. Cleveland Clinic and Mayo Clinic describe causes such as:

- **Problems in the testicles,** such as injury, infection, or effects of chemotherapy or radiation
- **Problems with the pituitary gland or hypothalamus,** the parts of the brain that signal the testicles to make testosterone
- **Certain medicines,** including opioid pain medicines and some steroids
- **Obesity and type 2 diabetes,** which are both linked to lower testosterone
- **Sleep apnea,** the breathing problem during sleep that often shows up as loud snoring. Our guide to [sleep apnea in men](/mens-health/sleep-apnea-in-men/) has a screening checklist
- **Some genetic conditions,** such as Klinefelter syndrome
- **Serious illness and heavy alcohol use**

### If you carry extra weight around your middle

Losing some belly fat is one of the changes doctors often suggest, and it helps your heart and blood sugar at the same time. Our guide to [belly fat in men](/mens-health/belly-fat-in-men/) covers realistic first steps.

### If you are trying to have children

Say so early. Testosterone therapy can lower sperm production, so your doctor may choose a different approach.

### If you take opioids or other long-term medicines

Don't stop anything on your own. Ask whether your medicine could be affecting your hormones and whether there are alternatives.

## Testosterone therapy is a prescription decision with risks

When testosterone is truly low and causing symptoms, a doctor may discuss testosterone therapy. Mayo Clinic notes that the benefits are much less clear for healthy men whose levels have dropped only through normal aging.

Possible risks and side effects that Mayo Clinic and the Urology Care Foundation describe include:

- Lower sperm production and smaller testicles
- Acne or other skin reactions
- A rise in red blood cells, which can raise the risk of blood clots
- Worsening of sleep apnea
- Breast enlargement
- Effects on the prostate, which is why doctors monitor it during treatment

Therapy also needs regular follow-up blood tests. That's one reason it should only come from a doctor who diagnosed you.

We don't cover products sold as "testosterone boosters." If you're thinking about trying one, show the label to your doctor or pharmacist first, since supplements can interact with medicines.

## Questions to ask your doctor about low testosterone

- Should my testosterone be tested in the morning, and will we repeat it?
- Could sleep, weight, a medicine, or another condition explain my symptoms?
- If my level is low, what's causing it?
- Should I be checked for sleep apnea, diabetes, or thyroid problems?
- What are the risks and benefits of treatment for someone like me?
- How would treatment affect fertility, and how would we monitor it?

## When low T symptoms need a doctor soon

Book an appointment soon if you have:

- Breast growth or a lump in the breast or testicle
- Loss of body hair or testicles that seem smaller
- Hot flashes or sweats you can't explain
- Erection problems along with chest pain or shortness of breath during activity
- Headaches or vision changes along with low sex drive

**Call 911** for chest pain, pressure, or sudden shortness of breath.
