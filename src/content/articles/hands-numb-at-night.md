It's 2 a.m. and your hand is asleep, but you're not. The fingers feel thick and buzzing, as if they belong to someone else. You shake your hand, flex your fingers, and slowly the feeling comes back. Then it happens again the next night.

**Numb or tingling hands at night are most often caused by pressure on a nerve, and the most common culprit is carpal tunnel syndrome, where the median nerve gets squeezed in the wrist.** Which fingers go numb is your best clue. Thumb, index, and middle fingers point to the wrist. Little and ring fingers point to the elbow. Tingling that starts in the neck points higher up. This guide helps you sort that out and shows what to try tonight.

## What carpal tunnel is

The carpal tunnel is a narrow passage on the palm side of your wrist, made of bones and a tough band of tissue. The median nerve runs through it, along with the tendons that bend your fingers. When that space gets crowded, for example from swelling, the nerve gets squeezed.

The National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke (NINDS) describes the classic symptoms as burning, tingling, or itching numbness in the palm and the thumb, index, and middle fingers. Mayo Clinic notes that symptoms often wake people at night, and that many people "shake out" their hands to relieve them.

### Why it's worse at night

Many people sleep with their wrists curled, which narrows the tunnel for hours. That's why a splint that keeps the wrist straight at night is one of the first things doctors suggest.

## Read your fingers: which nerve is it?

| Where you feel it | Most likely nerve | Common cause | Often worse when… |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Thumb, index, middle, and half of the ring finger | Median nerve in the wrist | Carpal tunnel syndrome | Sleeping with bent wrists, typing, holding a phone or steering wheel |
| Little finger and half of the ring finger | Ulnar nerve at the elbow | Cubital tunnel syndrome | Sleeping with elbows bent, leaning on your elbows |
| Arm, hand, and fingers, with neck pain | A nerve root in the neck | Pinched nerve in the neck | Turning or tilting your head |
| Both hands and both feet, slowly spreading | Many small nerves | Peripheral neuropathy, for example from diabetes or low B12 | Often worse at night |
| One arm, with sudden weakness or a drooping face | Brain | Possible stroke | Any time. Call 911 |

The American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons notes that cubital tunnel syndrome often causes numbness in the ring and little fingers, especially when the elbow is bent for a long time, such as during sleep.

## Who tends to get carpal tunnel

Mayo Clinic lists several things that raise the risk. Tick the ones that apply:

- [ ] I'm a woman (the carpal tunnel tends to be smaller)
- [ ] I'm pregnant or recently was (fluid retention can add pressure)
- [ ] I have diabetes, rheumatoid arthritis, or a thyroid problem
- [ ] I'm carrying extra weight
- [ ] My work involves repeated wrist bending or vibrating tools
- [ ] I've had a wrist fracture or injury

## What to try tonight

- **Keep your wrist straight while you sleep.** A wrist splint from a pharmacy is a common first step. Ask your doctor or a hand therapist which type fits you.
- **Watch your sleep position.** Try not to sleep with your hands curled under your chin or your pillow, or with your elbows tightly bent.
- **Shake and stretch gently** when you wake up numb, then let your hands rest in a neutral position.
- **During the day, take short breaks** from typing, gripping, or phone use, and keep your wrists relaxed and straight.

## Starting points by situation

### If you're pregnant

Carpal tunnel symptoms are common during pregnancy and often ease after delivery. A night splint is a common, low-risk first step, and your doctor can check other causes.

### If you type or use tools all day

Breaks and a neutral wrist position matter as much as the splint. Keep your keyboard low enough that your wrists stay straight, not bent up.

### If you have diabetes

Numb hands can come from carpal tunnel or from nerve damage caused by diabetes, and sometimes both. Mention it at your next visit.

## When numb hands need a doctor

See a doctor if numbness:

- Happens most nights or continues during the day
- Comes with weakness, clumsiness, or dropping things
- Comes with shrinking of the muscles at the base of your thumb
- Doesn't improve after a few weeks with a night splint and daytime changes

Mayo Clinic notes that untreated carpal tunnel can lead to lasting nerve and muscle damage, so ongoing symptoms are worth checking.

**Call 911 right away** for sudden numbness or weakness in one arm or one side of the body, especially with face drooping, trouble speaking, or confusion. These can be signs of a stroke.

## Tonight's test

Before bed, notice exactly which fingers have been tingling, and write them down. If it's the thumb side, try keeping your wrists straight tonight, with a splint or simply by sleeping with your hands open and flat. If it's the little-finger side, try keeping your elbows straighter. Two or three nights of this tells you a lot, and the notes help your doctor if it continues.
