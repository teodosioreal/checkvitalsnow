The ringing isn't new anymore. You've had the hearing test, you know roughly why it's there, and you've heard the phrase "you'll have to learn to live with it." What nobody explained is how. Especially at 11 p.m., when the house is silent and the ringing seems to fill the whole room.

**For most people, long-term tinnitus can't be switched off, but it can become much less noticeable. The tools with the best support are background sound, hearing aids when there's hearing loss, cognitive behavioral therapy, and protecting your ears, along with better sleep and less stress.** Supplements marketed for tinnitus haven't been shown to help. Here's how to put the useful tools together, starting tonight.

## Why tinnitus is louder some days than others

Your brain decides what to pay attention to. Tinnitus tends to grab more attention when it's quiet, when you're stressed or anxious, when you're short on sleep, and when you're focused on it. That's why the same ringing can be barely there on a busy afternoon and overwhelming at bedtime. Most of what helps works by changing those conditions, so your brain gradually learns to treat the sound as unimportant background.

## What can help, and how well it's supported

NIDCD and Mayo Clinic describe several approaches. Most people do best with a mix:

| Approach | What it does | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Background sound (sound therapy) | Fans, soft music, or sound machines make the ringing less noticeable | Almost everyone, especially at night |
| Hearing aids | Make outside sounds easier to hear, so tinnitus stands out less | People with hearing loss, which is very common with tinnitus |
| Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) | Changes how you think and react to the sound, reducing distress | Tinnitus that affects mood, sleep, or focus |
| Tinnitus retraining therapy | Combines counseling with sound to help your brain tune out the ringing | Long-lasting, bothersome tinnitus |
| Treating a cause | Removing earwax, changing a medicine, treating a jaw problem | When a specific cause is found |
| Protecting your hearing | Earplugs and lower volumes prevent more damage | Everyone with tinnitus |

## Your tonight routine for quieter nights

Silence is tinnitus's best friend. Try this for a week:

1. **Pick a sound source:** a fan, a white or pink noise machine, a nature-sound app, or soft music.
2. **Set the volume just below your tinnitus**, so it blends with the ringing rather than covering it completely. This helps your brain learn to tune both out.
3. **Keep a regular bedtime and wake time**, even on weekends.
4. **Skip caffeine after midday and limit alcohol** in the evening. Both can disrupt sleep, and some people notice tinnitus more after them.
5. **Wind down for 20 to 30 minutes** with something calm: reading, stretching, slow breathing.
6. **If you can't sleep after about 20 minutes**, get up, do something quiet in dim light, and return when sleepy.

## Calming the stress cycle

Tinnitus and stress feed each other. A few practical ways to break the loop:

- **Slow breathing:** in for a count of four, out for a count of six, for a few minutes when the ringing feels loud.
- **Move most days.** Walks, swimming, or cycling help with both stress and sleep.
- **Limit "checking."** Testing whether the ringing is still there keeps your attention on it. Shift to an activity instead.
- **Ask about CBT.** It's one of the best-studied ways to reduce how much tinnitus bothers people, and it can be done in person or online with a trained therapist.

## What about supplements?

Search for tinnitus relief and you'll find ginkgo, zinc, magnesium, and "ear health" blends. The National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health notes that ginkgo hasn't been shown to help tinnitus, and it can interact with blood thinners. There's no strong evidence that other supplements make tinnitus go away. If you want to try something, check with your doctor or pharmacist first, especially if you take other medicines.

## Protect what you have

NIDCD warns that long or repeated exposure to sounds at or above 85 decibels can damage hearing, and more damage can make tinnitus worse. Simple rules:

- Wear earplugs at concerts, sporting events, and with power tools or mowers.
- Keep headphone volume at a level where you could still hear someone talking to you.
- Give your ears quiet breaks after loud events.

## A four-week plan

| Week | Focus | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Nights | Start the tonight routine with background sound every night |
| 2 | Hearing | Book a hearing test if you haven't had one, and lower headphone volume |
| 3 | Stress | Add slow breathing and a daily walk |
| 4 | Next level | If tinnitus still affects your sleep or mood, ask your doctor about CBT or tinnitus retraining |

Rate your tinnitus distress from 0 to 10 at the end of each week, not just the loudness. Many people find the ringing stays about the same while the distress drops, and that's what makes life feel normal again.

## When to go back to your doctor

See your doctor if your tinnitus changes: it gets louder, moves to one ear, starts pulsing with your heartbeat, or comes with dizziness or hearing loss. **Get help right away** for sudden hearing loss in one ear, or if tinnitus is affecting your mood to the point of hopelessness. In the U.S., call or text 988 to reach the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline.
