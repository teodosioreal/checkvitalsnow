The first steps out of bed are the worst. Your knees feel rusty, your fingers won't quite close into a fist, and getting down the stairs takes a hand on the rail. By mid-morning you're moving fine, but every morning starts the same way, and you're starting to wonder what's behind it.

**Some stiffness after a night of not moving is normal, especially as we get older. How long it lasts is your best clue. Stiffness that eases within about half an hour of moving often comes from osteoarthritis or simple inactivity. Stiffness that lasts longer, with swelling in several joints, can point to inflammatory arthritis, such as rheumatoid arthritis, which needs a doctor's care.** This guide helps you read your own pattern, gives you a 10-minute morning routine, and lists the signs that shouldn't wait.

## Why joints stiffen overnight

Joints are cushioned by cartilage and lubricated by fluid. When you don't move for hours, the joint and the tissues around it can feel tight, and moving helps them loosen up again. With age, cartilage wears and tissues lose some flexibility, so the stiffness becomes more noticeable. Inflammation in the joint lining adds swelling and makes stiffness last longer.

## What the length of your stiffness can tell you

| Clue | More like osteoarthritis (wear-related) | More like inflammatory arthritis (such as rheumatoid arthritis) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How long morning stiffness lasts | Usually eases within about 30 minutes of moving | Often lasts longer, sometimes hours |
| Which joints | Often knees, hips, hands, lower back, or neck, sometimes one side more | Often the small joints of the hands and feet, the same joints on both sides |
| Swelling | Sometimes, bony enlargement | Often soft, warm, tender swelling |
| Other symptoms | Pain that's worse with use, better with rest | Fatigue, sometimes low-grade fever, feeling unwell |
| Typical age at start | Usually middle age and older | Often 30 to 60, but any age |

NIAMS describes osteoarthritis as a disease in which the tissues in the joint break down over time, and rheumatoid arthritis as an autoimmune disease in which the immune system attacks the joints, often affecting the same joints on both sides of the body.

### Other causes to keep in mind

- **Polymyalgia rheumatica**, which NIAMS describes as causing pain and stiffness in the shoulders, neck, and hips, mostly in people over 50, often worst in the morning
- **Overuse** from a new activity or a long day on your feet
- **An underactive thyroid**, which can cause joint and muscle stiffness along with tiredness and feeling cold

## A 10-minute morning loosening routine

Do this before you get out of bed and right after. Move gently, never into sharp pain.

| Minutes | Where | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–2 | In bed | Ankle circles, then bend and straighten each knee slowly, 10 times each |
| 2–4 | In bed | Open and close your hands into a gentle fist, then spread your fingers wide, 10 times |
| 4–6 | Sitting on the bed's edge | Shoulder rolls and slow neck turns side to side |
| 6–10 | Shower | A warm shower. Keep moving your hands, wrists, and shoulders gently in the warm water |

Warmth helps many people. Some find a warm paraffin or water soak useful for stiff hands.

## Habits that can make mornings easier

- **Stay active during the day.** NIAMS notes that physical activity is an important part of managing osteoarthritis. Walking, cycling, and water exercise are gentle on joints.
- **Keep a healthy weight**, which reduces load on knees and hips.
- **Break up long sitting** in the evening, since stiffness builds the longer you're still.
- **Stretch gently before bed.**
- **Look at what you eat.** Our next guide on [anti-inflammatory eating](/pain-and-nerves/anti-inflammatory-eating/) shows the eating pattern linked with lower inflammation and better overall health.

## Stiffness patterns and where to start

### If you're over 50 and it's mostly knees, hips, or hands

Osteoarthritis is common. The morning routine and daily activity help most people, and your doctor can discuss pain relief and physical therapy.

### If it's the same small joints on both hands, with swelling

That pattern is worth checking for rheumatoid arthritis soon. Early treatment can help protect joints.

### If your shoulders and hips are stiff and you're over 50

Ask about polymyalgia rheumatica, especially if it came on fairly quickly.

### If you're young and active

Morning stiffness after hard training is common. If it lasts long, comes with swelling, or affects your lower back and improves with exercise, mention it to a doctor.

## Warning signs that need a doctor

See a doctor if:

- Morning stiffness lasts more than an hour on most days
- Joints are swollen, warm, or tender
- Stiffness comes with fatigue, fever, rash, or weight loss
- It's getting worse or limiting what you can do

**Get urgent care** for a joint that becomes suddenly hot, red, and very painful, especially with fever, since that can signal an infection or gout. If you're over 50 and have shoulder or hip stiffness along with a new headache, jaw pain when chewing, or vision changes, seek care right away.
