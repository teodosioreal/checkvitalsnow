"You stopped breathing again last night." If you've heard that sentence from the person who shares your bed, you are far from the only man who has. Most men with sleep apnea have no idea anything happens at night. They just know they wake up tired, and their partner has started sleeping in the guest room.

**The most common sleep apnea symptoms in men are loud snoring, pauses in breathing that end with a snort or gasp, and heavy sleepiness during the day.** Morning headaches, a dry mouth on waking, and getting up to urinate at night often come along too. It is a common, treatable breathing problem, not a sign that you are lazy or out of shape, and a sleep study can tell you for sure.

Below is a checklist your partner can fill in over a few nights, a plain explanation of how sleep studies work, and the signs that mean you shouldn't wait.

## What happens in your throat during sleep apnea

In **obstructive sleep apnea**, the most common type, the muscles in the back of the throat relax during sleep and the airway narrows or closes. Air stops moving for a few seconds or longer. Your brain senses the drop in oxygen and briefly wakes you, often with a snort or gasp, so you can breathe again. Then you fall back asleep without remembering it.

The National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute (NHLBI) explains that this cycle can repeat many times an hour. You may spend eight hours in bed and still get very little deep, restful sleep. There is also a less common type, **central sleep apnea**, where the brain doesn't send steady signals to breathe. It has different causes, and a sleep study tells the two apart.

## Sleep apnea symptoms in men, day and night

Some signs only another person can see. Others you feel yourself the next day.

| At night (others notice) | In the morning | During the day |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Loud, regular snoring | Headache | Strong sleepiness, nodding off while reading or watching TV |
| Breathing pauses, then a gasp, choke, or snort | Dry mouth or sore throat | Trouble focusing or remembering things |
| Restless tossing and turning | Feeling unrefreshed after a full night | Irritability or low mood |
| Waking up to urinate more than usual | | Drowsiness behind the wheel |

MedlinePlus and NHLBI list these as common symptoms. None of them proves sleep apnea on its own. Many men snore without having it, and tiredness has plenty of other causes. The pattern matters: snoring **plus** observed pauses **plus** daytime sleepiness is a strong reason to get checked.

If you also wake up several times a night to pee, that can be part of the picture. Our guide to [peeing at night](/mens-health/peeing-at-night-men/) covers the other reasons that happens.

## Why men are more likely to have sleep apnea

NHLBI lists several risk factors for obstructive sleep apnea. Some you can change and some you can't:

- **Being male.** Sleep apnea is more common in men than in women.
- **Older age.** Risk rises as you get older.
- **Overweight or obesity.** Extra tissue around the neck and upper airway can narrow it. Weight around the middle matters too, which we cover in our guide to [belly fat in men](/mens-health/belly-fat-in-men/).
- **Family history** and the shape of your jaw, tongue, tonsils, or airway.
- **Smoking and alcohol.** Smoking can inflame the airway, and alcohol and some sedating medicines relax the throat muscles even more.
- **Other conditions**, such as high blood pressure and type 2 diabetes, which often show up alongside sleep apnea.

Thin and younger men can have sleep apnea too. A narrow airway or large tonsils can be enough.

If you sleep alone, a phone voice-recording app left running for the first hour of sleep can capture snoring and gasping. It won't diagnose anything, but it gives your doctor something real to go on.

### The kind of questions a doctor may ask

Clinicians often use short screening questionnaires to decide who needs a sleep study. One widely used type asks about snoring, tiredness, whether anyone has seen you stop breathing, blood pressure, body weight, age, neck size, and sex. Each "yes" raises the likelihood of sleep apnea. You don't need to score yourself. If your checklist shows several of these, that's reason enough to book a visit.

## How sleep studies for apnea work

A sleep study is the only way to confirm sleep apnea. NHLBI describes two main options.

### In a sleep center (polysomnography)

You sleep one night in a private room at a sleep center or hospital. A technician places small sensors on your scalp, face, chest, and legs, and a clip on your finger. They record:

- Breathing and airflow
- Oxygen levels in your blood
- Heart rate and rhythm
- Brain activity, eye movements, and leg movements

It isn't painful. Most people sleep well enough for the test to work, even with the wires.

### At home

A **home sleep apnea test** uses a smaller device you wear in your own bed. It usually tracks breathing, airflow, and oxygen, but not brain activity. It can work well for adults whose doctor strongly suspects obstructive sleep apnea and who don't have certain other health problems. If the result is unclear, your doctor may still order a lab study.

### What happens after the test

A sleep specialist reviews the results and tells you whether you have sleep apnea and how severe it is. Treatment depends on the cause and severity. NHLBI and Mayo Clinic describe options such as:

- **A breathing device (positive airway pressure, or PAP)** that sends gentle air pressure through a mask to keep the airway open
- **A custom mouthpiece** from a dentist that moves the lower jaw forward
- **Lifestyle steps** like losing weight if needed, cutting back on alcohol, quitting smoking, and sleeping on your side
- **Surgery or implanted devices** for some people when other treatments don't work

Many men find the first weeks with a breathing machine awkward. Mask fit can be adjusted, so tell your care team if it bothers you instead of quietly giving up.

## Getting tested for sleep apnea: where to start

### If your partner is the one complaining

Show them the checklist above and ask for three nights of notes. Bring the notes to your next appointment. A doctor hears "my wife says I snore" all the time. "She saw me stop breathing twice in 15 minutes" gets a sleep study ordered.

### If you drive for work or long distances

Drowsy driving is one of the most immediate dangers of untreated sleep apnea. If you've caught yourself drifting out of your lane or can't remember the last few miles, tell your doctor soon. Pull over and rest when you feel sleepy.

### If you already have high blood pressure or diabetes

Ask your doctor directly whether you should be tested. These conditions often occur together, and treating sleep apnea is part of caring for your heart.

## How sleep apnea affects the heart

When breathing stops, oxygen levels drop and the body releases stress hormones that raise blood pressure and heart rate. Over years, NHLBI notes, untreated sleep apnea is linked to:

- High blood pressure
- Heart disease and heart failure
- Irregular heart rhythms such as atrial fibrillation
- Stroke
- Type 2 diabetes

This doesn't mean everyone who snores is headed for a heart attack. It means that sleep apnea is one of the things worth finding, alongside the blood pressure and cholesterol checks in our [men's health screenings by age](/mens-health/mens-health-screenings-by-age/) guide. Low energy from poor sleep can also be confused with hormone changes, so if you've been wondering about [low testosterone symptoms](/mens-health/low-testosterone-symptoms/), mention your sleep too.

## Sleep apnea warning signs that need quick attention

See a doctor soon if:

- Someone has seen you stop breathing during sleep
- You fall asleep during the day without meaning to, especially while driving
- Snoring comes with morning headaches and high blood pressure that is hard to control
- You wake up gasping or choking

**Call 911** if you have chest pain, sudden shortness of breath, fainting, or signs of a stroke such as face drooping, arm weakness, or slurred speech. Those are emergencies, whatever the cause.
