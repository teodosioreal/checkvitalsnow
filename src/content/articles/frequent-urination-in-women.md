You plan your errands around bathrooms. You check where the restroom is the moment you walk into a store. At night you wake up once, twice, sometimes more, to go again. And often, when you get there, not much comes out.

**Frequent urination means needing to pee more often than usual, and many experts put the line at more than about eight times in 24 hours.** In women, the most common reasons are an overactive bladder, a urinary tract infection, hormonal changes such as menopause, and everyday habits like caffeine intake. Some causes are simple to fix. Others need a doctor.

This guide covers the usual causes, the habits that help, and the warning signs to watch for.

## What counts as frequent urination?

Everyone is different, and how much you drink matters. Still, it may be worth a closer look if you:

- Urinate more than about **eight times** in 24 hours
- Wake up **more than once a night** to urinate
- Feel a **sudden, strong urge** that is hard to hold
- Often pass **small amounts** of urine

Frequent urination often comes with **urgency**, the feeling that you need to go right now. When urgency leads to leakage, it is called urge incontinence.

## Common causes in women

### Overactive bladder (OAB)

According to the Urology Care Foundation, overactive bladder causes a sudden urge to urinate that you can't control, frequent trips to the bathroom, and sometimes leakage. The bladder muscle squeezes at the wrong times, even when the bladder is not full.

### Urinary tract infection (UTI)

A UTI can cause frequent urination along with burning, pain, cloudy or strong-smelling urine, and pressure in the lower belly. UTIs need medical treatment, so don't wait if you notice these signs.

### Pregnancy and childbirth

During pregnancy, the growing uterus presses on the bladder. Childbirth can also weaken the pelvic floor muscles, which can affect bladder control later on.

### Menopause

Lower estrogen after menopause can affect the tissues of the bladder and urethra. Many women notice more urgency, more frequent trips, and more nighttime urination during this stage. Learn more in our guide to [menopause and bladder leakage](/womens-health/menopause-and-bladder-leakage/).

### Diabetes

MedlinePlus lists diabetes that is not well controlled as a cause of frequent urination. High blood sugar pulls extra water into the urine. Watch for unusual thirst, tiredness, and weight loss as well.

### Drinks, foods, and medicines

Caffeine and alcohol make the body produce more urine and can irritate the bladder. Carbonated drinks and artificial sweeteners bother some people too. Water pills (diuretics) are another common cause. See our list of [foods and drinks that can irritate the bladder](/womens-health/bladder-irritants-foods-and-drinks/).

### Other causes

MedlinePlus also lists vaginitis and interstitial cystitis (a painful bladder condition) among possible causes. This is why a check-up matters if the problem does not go away.

## Habits that can help

### Bladder training

Bladder training teaches your bladder to hold more urine for longer. Here is the basic method:

1. For a few days, keep a simple **bladder diary**: write down when you urinate and when you feel urges.
2. Find your usual time between bathroom trips, for example every 60 minutes.
3. Add **15 minutes** to that interval and go on a schedule, even if you don't feel the urge.
4. When an urge comes early, stay still, take slow breaths, and squeeze your pelvic floor muscles a few times until it passes.
5. Every week or so, add a little more time.

It takes patience, but many women see progress over a few weeks.

### Smart fluid habits

- **Don't cut water too much.** Concentrated urine can irritate the bladder and make urgency worse.
- **Spread your drinks through the day** instead of drinking a lot at once.
- **Drink less in the evening** if you wake up at night to urinate.
- **Cut back on caffeine and alcohol**, especially later in the day.

### Strengthen the pelvic floor

[Kegel exercises](/womens-health/kegel-exercises-for-women/) can help you hold back an urge long enough to reach the bathroom.

### Keep bowels regular

Constipation puts pressure on the bladder. Fiber, fluids, and regular movement help.

## When to see a doctor

See a doctor promptly if frequent urination comes with any of these:

- Pain or burning when you urinate
- Fever, chills, or back or side pain
- Blood in the urine, or urine that looks dark or cloudy
- Unusual thirst, hunger, tiredness, or unexplained weight loss
- Leakage you can't control
- Discharge from the vagina

Also make an appointment if frequent urination is affecting your sleep, work, or social life, even without other symptoms. There are effective treatments, including medicines, for overactive bladder.
