You turn to check your blind spot and stop halfway. One side of your neck grabs, sharp and tight, and now you're turning your whole body like a lighthouse. Maybe it started after a night on a strange pillow. Maybe it crept in over a week of long screen days. And maybe, more worryingly, there's a buzz running down into your arm.

**Neck pain on one side usually comes from a strained muscle and eases within days to a few weeks with simple care. When the pain spreads into your shoulder or arm with numbness, tingling, or weakness, a pinched nerve is more likely.** This guide helps you tell those apart, shows what to do in the first 48 hours, and lists the rare signs that mean you should get help right away.

## What usually strains one side of the neck

Mayo Clinic lists these common causes of neck pain:

- **Muscle strain**, often from hunching over a computer or phone, reading in bed, sleeping in an awkward position, or clenching your jaw
- **Worn joints**, since the neck joints wear with age, much like other joints
- **Nerve compression**, when a herniated disk or bone spur in the neck presses on a nerve root
- **Injuries**, such as whiplash from a rear-end car crash
- **Disease**, less often, such as rheumatoid arthritis or an infection

One-sided pain is most often muscular, because we tend to favor one side: the shoulder we carry a bag on, the side we sleep on, the direction we turn toward a second monitor.

## Muscle or pinched nerve? Read the clues

| Clue | More like a strained muscle | More like a pinched nerve (cervical radiculopathy) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where it hurts | Neck and top of the shoulder, one side | Neck pain that travels into the shoulder blade, arm, or hand |
| What it feels like | Tight, sore, knotted, hard to turn your head | Sharp, burning, electric, or "pins and needles" |
| Numbness or weakness | No | Often numbness or tingling in the arm or fingers, sometimes weakness |
| Worse with | Holding your head still too long, certain movements | Turning or tilting the head toward the painful side |
| Typical course | Better within days to a couple of weeks | Many people improve over weeks with care, but some need treatment |

The American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons describes cervical radiculopathy as a nerve in the neck that is compressed or irritated where it leaves the spinal column, causing pain that radiates into the arm along with possible numbness or weakness.

If your fingers go numb mostly at night, especially the thumb, index, and middle fingers, the cause may be in your wrist instead. Our guide on [why hands go numb at night](/pain-and-nerves/hands-numb-at-night/) explains how to tell.

## Your first 48 hours

Mayo Clinic's self-care advice for neck pain follows a simple pattern:

| When | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| First 48 hours | Ice wrapped in a cloth for about 15 to 20 minutes, several times a day |
| After 48 hours | Switch to heat: a warm shower, warm compress, or heating pad on low |
| Every day | Gentle movement. Slowly turn and tilt your head within a comfortable range. |
| As needed | Over-the-counter pain relievers, if they're safe for you |
| All day | Avoid long stretches in the same position. Get up and move every 30 to 60 minutes. |

Avoid wearing a soft neck collar for long periods unless a doctor tells you to. Keeping your neck still for days can make it stiffer.

### Two gentle movements to start with

- **Chin tucks:** sitting tall, slide your chin straight back, as if making a double chin, and hold for 5 seconds. Repeat 10 times. This works the deep muscles that support your neck.
- **Side tilt:** slowly tilt your ear toward your shoulder on the non-painful side until you feel a gentle stretch. Hold 20 to 30 seconds, then switch. Never force it.

Stop any movement that sends pain or tingling down your arm.

## Pick your starting point

### If you woke up with it

Look at your pillow. A pillow that's too high or too flat can bend your neck all night. Mayo Clinic suggests sleeping with your head and neck aligned with your body, with a small pillow under your neck.

### If you're on a screen most of the day

Your setup is likely part of the story. Our next guide on [tech neck](/pain-and-nerves/tech-neck-posture/) shows the daily posture habits that strain the neck and how to change them.

### If the pain runs into your arm

Rest from heavy lifting and overhead work, keep moving gently, and see a doctor if it lasts more than a few days or comes with weakness.

### If it started after a car accident or fall

Get checked before trying home care.

## When to get help

Mayo Clinic advises seeing a doctor if neck pain is severe, lasts several days without relief, spreads down your arms or legs, or comes with headache, numbness, weakness, or tingling.

**Call 911 or get emergency care if you have:**

- Neck pain after an injury, such as a car accident, fall, or diving accident
- A stiff neck with fever and headache, which can be a sign of a serious infection
- Neck or jaw pain with chest pressure, shortness of breath, nausea, or a cold sweat. The National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute lists jaw and neck pain among the possible symptoms of a heart attack.

## One thing to change today

Notice which side hurts, then look around: is that the side of your second monitor, your bag strap, or the arm you hold your phone with? Change one of those today, apply ice or heat depending on how long it has been, and do 10 chin tucks before bed.
