The lab results land in your patient portal before your doctor has called. There's a row of letters, LDL-C, HDL-C, TRIG, a column of numbers, and a bright red "H" next to one of them. Now you're searching what it means at 10 p.m.

Here is the plain-language version. **High cholesterol means you have more of certain fats in your blood than is healthy, most often LDL ("bad") cholesterol. MedlinePlus lists a healthy LDL level for adults as under 100 mg/dL.** Over time, high LDL can help plaque build up in your arteries, which raises the risk of heart attack and stroke.

A flagged number is common and it's a starting point, not a verdict. High cholesterol usually has no symptoms, so a blood test is how almost everyone finds out. Below is what each number means, a table to copy your results into, and the questions to bring to your doctor.

## What high cholesterol means, and why your body makes cholesterol at all

Cholesterol isn't all bad. Your body uses this waxy substance to build cells and make some hormones and vitamin D. Your liver makes all the cholesterol you need, and more comes from food, mostly animal products.

Cholesterol travels through the blood packed inside particles called lipoproteins. The type of particle decides what happens next, and that's why your report has more than one number.

## LDL, HDL, and triglycerides in plain words

| What the report says | What it is | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **LDL** (low-density lipoprotein) | Often called "bad" cholesterol | High levels can build up in artery walls and form plaque that narrows them |
| **HDL** (high-density lipoprotein) | Often called "good" cholesterol | Helps carry cholesterol from the rest of the body back to the liver, which removes it |
| **Triglycerides** | A different type of fat in the blood that stores extra energy from food | High levels, especially with high LDL or low HDL, are linked to heart disease |
| **Total cholesterol** | A combined measure of cholesterol in your blood | Gives a broad picture but doesn't show the balance between types |
| **Non-HDL cholesterol** | Total cholesterol minus HDL | Captures all the cholesterol types that can contribute to plaque |

One way to remember it: **L**DL is the one you want **L**ow, and **H**DL is the one you want **H**igher.

## Healthy cholesterol numbers for adults, according to MedlinePlus

MedlinePlus lists these healthy levels for adults age 20 and older. All numbers are in mg/dL (milligrams per deciliter).

| Test | Healthy level for adults |
| --- | --- |
| Total cholesterol | 125 to 200 |
| Non-HDL cholesterol | Less than 130 |
| LDL cholesterol | Less than 100 |
| HDL cholesterol | 40 or higher (men), 50 or higher (women) |
| Triglycerides | Less than 150 (listed as normal on MedlinePlus's triglycerides page) |

**Your goal may differ.** The NHLBI explains that doctors look at cholesterol together with other things that raise heart risk, such as age, blood pressure, smoking, diabetes, and family history. Someone who already had a heart attack, for example, may be given a different target than someone with no other risk factors. Ask your doctor what your personal numbers should be.

## How often to get your cholesterol checked

The CDC says **most healthy adults should get their cholesterol checked every 4 to 6 years**. You may need more frequent tests if you have heart disease, diabetes, or a family history of high cholesterol, or if your last results were high. The CDC also recommends testing for children and teens, so ask your child's doctor about timing.

The test itself is a simple blood draw called a lipid panel. Some panels are done without fasting, and some ask you to fast first. Ask your doctor's office which one you're getting.

### Questions to bring to your cholesterol appointment

- Based on my overall risk, what numbers should I aim for?
- Could any of my medicines, or another condition like thyroid problems or diabetes, be affecting these results?
- Should anyone else in my family get tested?
- Would lifestyle changes be enough to start, or do you recommend medicine too?
- When should I repeat this test?

## Paths by profile: what your cholesterol results may mean for you

### If high cholesterol runs in your family

A condition called **familial hypercholesterolemia** is passed down through families and causes very high LDL from a young age. The CDC notes that people with it may have early heart disease. If close relatives had high cholesterol or heart attacks at a young age, tell your doctor, since you and your children may need earlier testing.

### If your LDL is fine but triglycerides are high

Triglycerides are closely tied to what you eat and drink. MedlinePlus lists eating too many calories, especially from sugar and refined carbs, drinking a lot of alcohol, and some conditions such as diabetes as possible causes. Your doctor may check for these.

### If you also have high blood pressure or diabetes

These conditions add to heart risk alongside cholesterol. Ask your doctor how all your numbers fit together. Our guide on [how to lower blood pressure](/heart-health/how-to-lower-blood-pressure/) covers habits that may help both, and the [blood pressure chart](/heart-health/blood-pressure-readings-chart/) explains your other key number.

### If you've been prescribed a cholesterol medicine

Take it exactly as prescribed, and don't stop it on your own. If you have side effects, such as muscle aches, tell your doctor. There may be other options.

## Food and habit swaps for healthier cholesterol

The NHLBI recommends heart-healthy lifestyle changes as a core part of managing cholesterol. These swaps follow that approach. They may help, but results vary, and they don't replace medicine if your doctor recommends it.

| Swap this... | For this... | Why it may help |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Butter or lard for cooking | Olive, canola, or other vegetable oils | Cuts saturated fat |
| Fatty cuts of red meat, sausage, bacon | Fish, skinless poultry, beans, or lentils | Cuts saturated fat, adds protein |
| Full-fat cheese and whole milk | Low-fat or fat-free dairy | Cuts saturated fat |
| White bread, sugary cereal | Oatmeal, whole-grain bread, barley | Adds soluble fiber |
| Pastries, fried snacks | Fruit, a small handful of unsalted nuts | Fewer trans and saturated fats, more fiber |
| Sugary drinks or several alcoholic drinks | Water or unsweetened drinks | May help with triglycerides |
| Evenings on the couch | A brisk walk after dinner | Regular activity may help raise HDL and lower triglycerides |

Not smoking matters too. Quitting can help your HDL and your blood vessels.

## When cholesterol concerns turn into heart warning signs

High cholesterol itself doesn't cause symptoms. But the plaque it contributes to can narrow arteries over years, and that can lead to serious problems.

**Call 911 right away** if you or someone else has:

- Chest pain, pressure, squeezing, or discomfort, especially if it spreads to the arm, back, neck, or jaw
- Shortness of breath, cold sweat, nausea, or lightheadedness with chest discomfort
- Sudden face drooping, arm weakness, or trouble speaking, which can be signs of a stroke

**See your doctor soon** if you notice:

- Chest discomfort or shortness of breath that comes on with activity and eases with rest
- Pain or cramping in your legs when you walk that goes away when you stop
- Yellowish, fatty bumps on the skin, especially around the eyes, elbows, or knees, which can be seen in people with very high cholesterol

Log in to your patient portal or call your doctor's office today and find your most recent cholesterol results. Copy them into the table above, along with the date. If it's been more than a few years, or you've never had one, ask for a test at your next visit.
