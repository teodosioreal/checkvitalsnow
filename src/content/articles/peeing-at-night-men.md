11:40 p.m. Lights out. 2:15 a.m. Up. 4:50 a.m. Up again. By the time the alarm goes off, the night has been cut into three short pieces, and the first thought of the day is coffee.

**Waking up at night to pee, called nocturia, has many causes in men besides the prostate. Drinks late in the evening, alcohol and caffeine, sleep apnea, diabetes, fluid that collects in the legs during the day, and certain medicines can all send you to the bathroom at night.** Finding your cause is the key to fewer trips, and a simple three-night diary is the fastest way to start.

Nighttime bathroom trips are common, and they get more common with age. Still, MedlinePlus explains that the body normally makes less urine at night, which lets most people sleep six to eight hours without getting up. When that pattern changes, there is usually a reason worth looking for.

## Two ways nighttime urination happens

Doctors think about nocturia in two broad ways. Cleveland Clinic describes both, and knowing which one fits you narrows the causes a lot.

- **Your body makes too much urine at night.** The bladder is fine, but the kidneys keep producing. Evening fluids, alcohol, diabetes, and fluid shifting out of swollen legs when you lie down fit here.
- **Your bladder can't hold much, or doesn't empty well.** You make a normal amount, but you have to go before it's full. An enlarged prostate, an overactive bladder, and infections fit here.

Many men have a bit of both. The diary below shows which side is bigger for you.

## Causes of peeing at night in men, beyond the prostate

The table pairs each common cause with clues and a sensible next step. MedlinePlus and Cleveland Clinic list these among the causes of nighttime urination.

| Possible cause | Clues that point to it | Next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drinks in the evening | Large drinks with or after dinner, water at the bedside | Move most fluids earlier in the day |
| Alcohol or caffeine | Worse after a beer, wine, coffee, tea, or soda later in the day | Cut back in the evening and watch the diary |
| Sleep apnea | Loud snoring, gasping, morning headaches, daytime sleepiness | Ask about a sleep evaluation |
| Diabetes | Peeing a lot day and night, unusual thirst, weight loss, blurry vision | Ask for a blood sugar test |
| Fluid in the legs | Swollen ankles by evening that look better by morning | See a doctor, and try raising your legs in the late afternoon |
| Heart failure | Leg swelling plus shortness of breath or tiredness with activity | See a doctor soon |
| Medicines, especially water pills (diuretics) | Started or changed a medicine around the time this began | Ask whether the dose time can move earlier |
| Enlarged prostate (BPH) | Weak stream, hard to start, feeling you didn't finish | See our guide to [enlarged prostate treatment](/mens-health/enlarged-prostate-back-to-normal/) |
| Prostatitis or urinary infection | Burning, pelvic pain, fever | See our comparison of [prostatitis vs. an enlarged prostate](/mens-health/prostatitis-vs-enlarged-prostate/) |

### Why swollen legs can wake you up

This one surprises many men. If you stand or sit for much of the day, fluid can settle in your lower legs. When you lie down, that fluid moves back into the bloodstream, and the kidneys turn it into urine, often in the first hours of sleep. Swelling in the legs can also be a sign of heart, kidney, or vein problems, so it deserves a checkup on its own.

### Why sleep apnea and nighttime urination go together

In obstructive sleep apnea, the airway repeatedly narrows or closes during sleep. MedlinePlus lists sleep apnea among the causes of nighttime urination, and the National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute (NHLBI) describes loud snoring and daytime sleepiness as common signs of the condition. If you snore and wake up to pee, the bathroom may not be the main problem. Our guide to [sleep apnea in men](/mens-health/sleep-apnea-in-men/) explains the warning signs and testing.

### Why diabetes can show up at night first

High blood sugar pulls extra water into the urine. NIDDK lists increased urination, including at night, along with increased thirst, as common symptoms of diabetes. A simple blood test can check.

## Your three-night bladder diary

This is the single most useful thing you can bring to an appointment about nighttime urination. Keep it for three days and nights in a row. A measuring cup or a marked plastic container kept by the toilet makes it easy to estimate amounts. If measuring isn't practical, write small, medium, or large.

| Time | Drink (type and amount) | Peed (amount) | Woke you up? | Urge level (0 to 3) | Notes (swollen ankles, snoring, medicine taken) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 7:30 p.m. | 2 beers with dinner | | | | Ankles a little puffy |
| 10:45 p.m. | | Medium | No, before bed | 1 | Took water pill this morning |
| 2:10 a.m. | | Large | Yes | 2 | Partner says I snored |
| | | | | | |

### How to read your diary

- **Large amounts at night** point toward your body making too much urine. Look at evening drinks, swelling, sleep apnea, blood sugar, and medicine timing.
- **Small amounts with a strong urge** point toward the bladder or prostate side.
- **Small amounts with no real urge** can mean you're waking for another reason, such as apnea or light sleep, and peeing because you're up.
- **Lots of urine day and night**, with thirst, is worth a blood sugar check soon.

Bring the diary and your medicine list to your appointment. It often saves a second visit.

## Changes that may help, matched to the cause

### If evening drinks are a big part of it

Front-load your fluids. Drink most of your water before late afternoon and keep evening drinks small. Don't cut your daily total, since mild dehydration can bring other problems. If your doctor has set a fluid amount for you because of heart or kidney disease, follow that plan instead.

### If alcohol or caffeine shows up in your diary

Try a two-week experiment: no alcohol or caffeine after mid-afternoon. Compare the diary before and after.

### If your ankles swell by evening

Raise your legs for an hour or so in the late afternoon or early evening, so fluid moves and you pee before bed rather than after. Ask your doctor whether compression stockings make sense for you. Get the swelling checked, too.

### If you take a water pill

Ask your doctor or pharmacist whether you can take it earlier in the day. Don't change the timing or stop it on your own.

### If you snore or wake up tired

Treat this as a priority. Ask for a sleep evaluation instead of only focusing on the bladder.

### Small habits for every man

- Pee right before bed, and again if you're still awake 15 minutes later.
- Keep the path to the bathroom clear and use a night light to lower the risk of falls.
- Keep a regular sleep schedule, since light, broken sleep makes you notice every urge.

## Nighttime urination signs that need prompt care

See a doctor soon if you have:

- A weak stream, straining, or feeling that your bladder never empties
- Burning, pelvic pain, or fever with urination
- Blood in your urine
- Unusual thirst, unexplained weight loss, or blurry vision
- Swelling in your legs with shortness of breath, or needing extra pillows to breathe at night
- Loud snoring with pauses in breathing or gasping

**Call 911 or go to the emergency room** if you can't urinate at all, or if you have chest pain or sudden, severe shortness of breath.

## Start the diary tonight

Put a pen, the diary, and a cup by the toilet before you go to bed tonight. Three nights from now, you'll know whether your problem is how much urine you make or how much your bladder holds, and that answer points your doctor in the right direction from the first visit.
