Think of erectile dysfunction less as a private problem and more as a dashboard warning light. The arteries that supply the penis are small, so when blood vessels start to narrow or stiffen, this is often one of the first places it shows.

**The most common erectile dysfunction causes are physical: conditions that damage blood vessels and nerves, such as heart disease, high blood pressure, high cholesterol, and diabetes. Medicines, smoking, heavy drinking, low testosterone, stress, anxiety, and depression can also cause or worsen ED.** Because of the blood vessel link, ED can be an early signal of heart or metabolic disease, which makes it worth a real checkup instead of a quick online order.

ED is common, especially with age, and it is a medical condition like any other. Bringing it up with a doctor is routine for them, and the conversation can protect far more than your sex life.

## How erections depend on blood vessels and nerves

An erection happens when nerve signals relax the muscles in the blood vessels of the penis, letting blood flow in and stay there. NIDDK explains that ED can result from problems at any stage: the nerves, the blood vessels, the hormones involved, or the brain's role in arousal.

That's why so many different health conditions can show up as ED. Anything that narrows arteries, damages nerves, or changes hormone signals can get in the way.

## ED and heart disease: why doctors check your heart

NIDDK lists heart and blood vessel disease, atherosclerosis (hardened, narrowed arteries), and high blood pressure among the conditions that can cause ED. Mayo Clinic notes that ED can be a sign of an underlying health condition, including heart disease.

In practice, this means a doctor who hears "ED" may also want to check:

- Blood pressure
- Cholesterol
- Blood sugar
- Weight and waist size
- Smoking history and physical activity

If ED is your first sign of a blood vessel problem, finding it early gives you a chance to protect your heart before you ever feel chest symptoms.

## Diabetes and erectile dysfunction

NIDDK lists diabetes among the leading causes of ED. Over time, high blood sugar can damage both the nerves and the small blood vessels that erections depend on. Men with diabetes are more likely to have ED, and it may show up at a younger age.

If you have ED along with frequent urination, unusual thirst, or tiredness, ask for a blood sugar test. If you already have diabetes, ED is worth mentioning at your next diabetes visit, since keeping blood sugar, blood pressure, and cholesterol in range protects these same vessels and nerves.

## Other causes of ED, from medicines to stress

The table below groups the main causes that NIDDK, Mayo Clinic, and Cleveland Clinic describe, with a practical next step for each.

| Cause | Examples | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blood vessel conditions | Heart disease, high blood pressure, high cholesterol, atherosclerosis | Get blood pressure, cholesterol, and heart risk checked |
| Diabetes and metabolic health | Type 2 diabetes, obesity | Ask for a blood sugar test. Our guide to [belly fat in men](/mens-health/belly-fat-in-men/) covers waist-related risk |
| Medicines | Some blood pressure medicines, antidepressants, and other prescriptions | Ask your doctor whether a medicine could be involved. Don't stop it on your own |
| Hormones | Low testosterone, thyroid problems | Ask about hormone tests. See our guide to [low testosterone symptoms](/mens-health/low-testosterone-symptoms/) |
| Nerve conditions and surgery | Multiple sclerosis, Parkinson's disease, spinal cord injury, prostate or bladder surgery | Talk with the specialist who treats the condition |
| Lifestyle | Smoking, heavy alcohol use, little physical activity | Changes here may help and also protect your heart |
| Sleep | Sleep apnea and short sleep | Ask about a sleep evaluation if you snore. See our guide to [sleep apnea in men](/mens-health/sleep-apnea-in-men/) |
| Emotional factors | Stress, anxiety, depression, relationship problems | Talk to your doctor or a mental health professional |

### Why stress and physical causes often travel together

Many men have a physical cause and then start worrying about the next time, which makes things harder. The worry is a real factor, not "all in your head." Addressing both sides tends to work better than focusing on just one.

## Your ED risk checklist

Check every line that applies. This isn't a diagnosis. It shows your doctor where to look first, and each checked line is a health issue worth working on for its own sake.

- [ ] I have high blood pressure, or I haven't had it checked in the past year
- [ ] I have high cholesterol, or I don't know my numbers
- [ ] I have diabetes or prediabetes, or I haven't had blood sugar checked recently
- [ ] I smoke or use tobacco
- [ ] I drink alcohol heavily
- [ ] I carry extra weight, especially around my waist
- [ ] I'm physically active less than most days of the week
- [ ] I snore loudly or feel sleepy during the day
- [ ] I take medicines for blood pressure, depression, or other long-term conditions
- [ ] I've had prostate, bladder, or pelvic surgery or radiation
- [ ] I've been under heavy stress, or I feel down or anxious most days
- [ ] I have low sex drive, low energy, or other signs of low testosterone

**Several checks in the first seven lines?** Ask your doctor for a heart and metabolic checkup, not only a conversation about ED.

## Lifestyle steps that may help with ED

NIDDK suggests that changes like these may help some men with ED, and they also help the heart and blood vessels:

- **Quit smoking.** Smoking damages blood vessels. Free help is available at 1-800-QUIT-NOW.
- **Be more active.** Regular physical activity supports blood flow and helps with weight and blood pressure.
- **Work toward a healthy weight,** especially if you carry it around the middle.
- **Limit alcohol.**
- **Look after sleep and stress.** Treating sleep apnea, depression, or anxiety can make a difference.

Results vary, and these steps don't replace a medical evaluation. They're worth doing whatever the cause.

### Paths by age and situation

**If you're under 40:** Physical causes are still possible, but stress, anxiety, alcohol, and recreational drugs may play a larger role in many younger men. A checkup is still worthwhile, especially if you smoke or have a family history of early heart disease.

**If you're over 50 with other health conditions:** Treat ED as a reason to review your heart, blood sugar, and medicines together.

**If ED started after a new medicine:** Write down when you started it and ask your prescriber about it.

**If you've had prostate treatment:** Ask your urologist about rehabilitation options. ED after prostate surgery or radiation is a known effect and can often be addressed.

## Why not to buy ED pills online without a prescription

Prescription ED medicines are effective for many men, but they aren't right for everyone. NIDDK and MedlinePlus warn that some ED medicines can be dangerous if taken with nitrates, which are medicines used for chest pain. A doctor also checks whether your heart is healthy enough for sexual activity and whether the medicine interacts with anything else you take.

Pills sold online without a prescription skip all of that, and you can't know what's actually in them. "Herbal" or "natural" products marketed for erections are no safer by default. If cost or embarrassment is the barrier, say so to your doctor. There are usually options.

## ED warning signs that need a doctor soon

Make an appointment soon if:

- ED is new and comes with chest pain, shortness of breath, or leg pain when you walk
- You have ED along with unusual thirst, frequent urination, or unexplained weight loss
- It started after an injury to the pelvis or back
- You also notice pain, a curve, or a lump in the penis
- You feel depressed or hopeless

**Get emergency care right away** if an erection lasts longer than four hours, a condition called priapism that needs urgent treatment. **Call 911** for chest pain or pressure, especially during or after sexual activity.
